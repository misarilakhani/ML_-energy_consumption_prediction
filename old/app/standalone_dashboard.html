Core Analytical Findings

  • Diurnal Surge: Daily consumption is concentrated during 17:00 - 20:00 (reaching 190.4 Wh average), driven by simultaneous cooking, lighting, and entertainment activities.
  • Short-Term Temporal Memory: Active appliance cycles persist across 20-60 minutes (2 to 6 consecutive 10-minute intervals). Past consumption lags (10m, 20m) provide the highest predictive importance.
  • Thermal Collinearity: Living room and kitchen temperatures correlate at r = 0.88, demonstrating substantial spatial thermal coupling.
  • Noise Feature Rejection: Artificial variables rv1 and rv2 correlated at r = -0.01 with consumption and were discarded.

Limitations & Academic Rigor

  • Single Household Context: Data originates from one low-energy residential house in Stambruges, Belgium monitored over 4.5 months. It cannot be applied to other climates or insulation classes without local retraining.
  • Correlation vs Causation: Observational ML demonstrates predictive association, not causal mechanics. Simulated recommendations are exploratory scenarios rather than guaranteed savings.
  • Abrupt Activation Limits: Unannounced sudden switch-ons of 800+ Wh appliances cannot be predicted in advance without behavioral pre-indicators.
