Problem Formulation & Context
Speaker 1: Problem Definition

The Engineering Challenge

Residential appliances (cooktops, washing machines, dryers) cause sudden electrical load surges that stress local grid transformers and drive peak electricity bills.

Core Task Formulation:

"Predict appliance energy consumption (Wh) for the next 10-minute interval ($t+1$) using only historical and environmental observations up to time $t$."

Information Boundary (Zero Lookahead)

  • Known at time $t$: Room temperatures ($T_1 \dots T_9$), humidities ($RH_1 \dots RH_9$), outdoor weather, historical consumption ($y_t, y_{t-1}, y_{t-6}$).
  • Unknown: Future consumption ($y_{t+1}$) and future weather/sensor conditions.
  • Impact: Enables automated Home Energy Management Systems (HEMS) to shift discretionary loads.
Dataset Description & Noise Audit
Speaker 1: Data Understanding

UCI Appliances Energy Dataset

  • Author: Luis Candanedo et al. (Univ. of Mons, Belgium, 2017).
  • Building: Low-energy single-family house in Stambruges, Belgium.
  • Span: 137 days (Jan 11, 2016 – May 27, 2016).
  • Observations: 19,735 rows logged at uniform 10-minute intervals.
  • Sensors: ZigBee network measuring 9 room temperatures and 9 room relative humidities.

Investigation of rv1 and rv2

The dataset contains two synthetic random variables (`rv1`, `rv2`).

Findings:
  • `rv1` and `rv2` are mathematically identical.
  • Correlation with appliance energy is $r = -0.011$ (pure noise).
  • Decision: Explicitly dropped during feature selection to protect generalization.
Preprocessing & Outlier Strategy
Speaker 2: Preprocessing

Quality & Chronological Splitting

  • Missing Values: Verified 0 missing values across all columns.
  • Zero Leakage Split: Strict temporal ordering:
    • Train: 70% (13,713 rows, Jan 12 – Apr 16)
    • Validation: 15% (2,938 rows, Apr 16 – May 07)
    • Test: 15% (2,939 rows, May 07 – May 27)
  • Scalers fitted strictly on training split only!

The Outlier & Balancing Debate

1. Outlier Decision:

Energy spikes up to $1080\text{ Wh}$ are legitimate physical appliance events (e.g., ovens, dryers). Deleting them would blind the system to peak demand.

2. Data Balancing in Regression:

SMOTE and classification resampling are mathematically inapplicable to continuous Wh regression. Tree ensembles natively manage right-skewed distributions.

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Speaker 2: EDA Insights

Diurnal Hourly Cycle

Marked daily swing: night trough at 03:00 ($48.2\text{ Wh}$) rising to a sharp evening cooking peak at 18:00 ($190.4\text{ Wh}$). Cyclical hour features are essential.

Day-of-Week Effect

Weekends exhibit slightly higher daytime baselines ($100.6\text{ Wh}$ vs $96.6\text{ Wh}$) due to sustained residential occupancy.

Multicollinearity in Sensors

Kitchen ($T_1$) and living room ($T_2$) correlate at $r = 0.84$. Regularized regression (Ridge) or tree ensembles are mandatory to handle high feature collinearity.

Spike Duration Continuity

Major loads persist across 20–60 minutes (2 to 6 consecutive 10-minute intervals). Past consumption lags provide powerful predictive continuity.

Feature Engineering Architecture
Speaker 3: Features & Baselines

49 Leak-Free Features Engineered

  • Cyclical Time: $\sin(2\pi h / 24), \cos(2\pi h / 24), \sin(\text{day}), \cos(\text{day})$.
  • Autoregressive Lags: Current load $y_t$, $10\text{m}$ ($y_{t-1}$), $20\text{m}$, $30\text{m}$, $1\text{h}$ ($y_{t-6}$), $24\text{h}$ ($y_{t-144}$).
  • Rolling Past Stats: 30-min mean & std, 1-hour mean & max (computed strictly on $\tau \le t$).
  • Domain Thermal Gradients: $T_1 - T_{out}$ (indoor/outdoor gradient), $T_1 - T_2$ (cooking thermal signature).

Baseline Benchmark Standards

1. Mean Target Baseline:

Predicts training mean ($97.7\text{ Wh}$). Validation RMSE: 90.46 Wh ($R^2 = -0.0057$).

2. Naive Persistence Baseline:

Predicts next = current draw ($\hat{y}_{t+1} = y_t$). Validation RMSE: 65.51 Wh ($R^2 = 0.4726$). A formidable reference!

Model Development & Tuning
Speaker 3: Machine Learning

Candidate ML Algorithms

Model 1 — Ridge Linear Regression:

L2-regularized pipeline with StandardScaler. Solves multicollinearity while retaining full interpretability.

Model 2 — Random Forest Regressor:

Ensemble of 150 bagged decision trees. Captures non-linear feature interactions.

Model 3 — Histogram Gradient Boosting:

Histogram-binned boosting. State-of-the-art efficiency and predictive accuracy.

Time-Series Tuning Strategy

Standard K-Fold randomly scrambles time and creates temporal leakage. We employed TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=3) exclusively on training data.

Best HistGB Hyperparameters:
  • Learning Rate: 0.05
  • Max Depth: 12
  • Min Samples Leaf: 15
  • L2 Regularization: 0.5

Diagnostic Error Dissection
Speaker 4: Error Analysis

Residual Distribution

Mean residual is $-2.80\text{ Wh}$ (close to 0), proving negligible bias. Heavy right tail in Q-Q plot reflects unannounced occupant activations.

Consumption Regime Breakdown

  • Standby ($\le 60\text{ Wh}$): MAE = 11.8 Wh (Highly precise).
  • Moderate ($60\text{--}200\text{ Wh}$): MAE = 32.4 Wh.
  • Peak Surge ($> 200\text{ Wh}$): MAE = 114.6 Wh.

Failure Case Audit

Why Predictions Fail:

The top 5 prediction failures occur during sudden unannounced switch-ons (e.g. baseline $50\text{ Wh}$ instantly surging to $820\text{ Wh}$). Observational sensor models cannot anticipate instantaneous human decisions without pre-event intent indicators.
